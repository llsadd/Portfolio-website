Flamingo Bus Miami

A tourism operator whose website made buying a ticket harder than turning up in person. They asked to fix one section - I advised to redesigned the whole website instead, which resulted in 5x increase in ticket bookings and 13% conversion rate.

RoleDesign lead, developer management
ScopeMulti-page redesign, print brochures, blog template
Tools Figma Midjourney Flux Analytics WordPress Adobe Illustrator Elementor
Year2024
Result3.5× active users
5× checkouts
13% conversion rate

01 — Problem02 — Research & decisions03 — Build04 — Outcome05 — Key Takeaway
01 — The problem

The brief was one section. Reading the site, the real problem was everything around it: huge blocks of white text on bright pink, no visual hierarchy, and a booking flow that most visitors abandoned. There was almost no usable photography either — a handful of low-quality assets.

I wrote up what I would change and why, and pitched a full redesign of the homepage first, then the rest of the site. That's what we ended up doing.

02 — Research & decisions
Competitor scanHow other Miami tour operators structure ticketing, what they lead with, where they ask for the sale.
One primary actionEvery page drives to booking. Routes, times and price surface above the fold; the copy got cut to what a tourist actually needs.
Imagery from nothingAI-generated graphics stood in for a photo library that didn't exist, matched to the new palette so the site read as one brand.
Content systemA blog post template so their SEO specialist could publish weekly without a designer, plus print brochures for on-bus sales.
03 — Build

Designed in Figma with adaptive desktop, tablet and mobile, then built with a developer I'd worked with before and managed through the whole implementation.

04 — Outcome
~3.5×
Active users, year on year
~5×
Completed checkouts

Google Analytics, same month year on year, with no paid campaigns running in either period.

05 — Key Takeaway

What this project proved is that seeing the real problem pays off: a client came in asking for one section fixed, and pitching the full redesign instead turned into the strongest results on my portfolio — active users up roughly 3.5× and completed checkouts up roughly 5× year over year, with no other marketing running in that window. Beyond the site itself, that same visual system carried through to print brochures, a blog template, and social graphics, so the whole brand felt like one coherent push, not just a redesigned homepage.
