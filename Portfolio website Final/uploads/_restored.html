How the work happens

The same eight steps whether it's a landing page or a full rebuild. Most of the value lands before anything is designed.

01BriefingA real discovery interview — two to three hours. Sometimes the client's own customers too.
02Competitor researchA dashboard of competitors: structure, funnel, messaging, lead-gen tactics, visual choices. Then what's worth reusing.
03Visual researchMood-boarding positioning and art direction before any pixels get pushed.
04Structure & funnelHero → what and why different → proof → process and CTA → FAQ → features → final CTA. One primary action per page.
05Brand identityPalette, typography, imagery style, iconography, layout language, illustration. Not logos — that gets delegated.
06Design system in FigmaReusable components — buttons, headers, footers, blocks — so the team can self-serve after I'm gone.
07BuildWix, Shopify, Webflow, Figma Sites, or WordPress with a developer I manage.
08AI contentMidjourney, Flux, Grok and Nano Banana for product photography and short ad creative when there's no shoot budget.

Flamingo Bus Miami

A tourism operator whose website made buying a ticket harder than turning up in person. Asked to fix one section; redesigned the whole thing instead.

01 — The problem

The brief was one section. Reading the site, the real problem was everything around it: huge blocks of white text on bright pink, no visual hierarchy, and a booking flow that most visitors abandoned. There was almost no usable photography either — a handful of low-quality assets.

I wrote up what I would change and why, and pitched a full redesign of the homepage first, then the rest of the site. That's what we ended up doing.

02 — Research & decisions
Competitor scanHow other Miami tour operators structure ticketing, what they lead with, where they ask for the sale.
One primary actionEvery page drives to booking. Routes, times and price surface above the fold; the copy got cut to what a tourist actually needs.
Imagery from nothingAI-generated graphics stood in for a photo library that didn't exist, matched to the new palette so the site read as one brand.
Content systemA blog post template so their SEO specialist could publish weekly without a designer, plus print brochures for on-bus sales.
03 — Build

Designed in Figma with adaptive desktop, tablet and mobile, then built with a developer I'd worked with before and managed through the whole implementation — including a client who checked pixel offsets against the Figma file.

1440×760 · booking flow, three steps — desktop and mobile side by side

Google Analytics, same month year on year, with no paid campaigns running in either period.

05 — What I'd do differently

After launch the client pushed a long run of incremental edits I disagreed with visually. I made the case several ways, held the line for a while, and eventually implemented most of them. The live version today is weaker than what shipped.

The lesson wasn't to fight harder. It was to tie design decisions to the metrics we already had, earlier — the same numbers above would have settled most of those arguments in a sentence.
